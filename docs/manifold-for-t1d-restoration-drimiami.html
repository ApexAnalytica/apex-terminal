Manifold for Type 1 Diabetes — Restoration

Diabetes Research Institute, University of Miami — long-horizon islet-transplant registry analytics and BioHub translation

1 · The clinical problem

The Diabetes Research Institute holds an asset that no other institution can replicate: the longest continuous programmatic record of clinical islet transplantation in the world, underpinned by the Ricordi Chamber isolation methodology that made the field's clinical arm reproducible. The DRI record spans:

  • Multi-decade allogeneic islet-transplant registry. Donor quality, isolation yield and viability, recipient HLA and autoantibody profile, immunosuppression regimen exposure, C-peptide AUC trajectory, insulin-dependence status, and long-horizon graft-survival outcomes across a patient population no other center matches.
  • Edmonton Protocol and post-Edmonton regimen variants. Longitudinal evidence base on immunosuppression-regimen modifications and their causal effect on graft durability — a natural domain for heterogeneous-treatment-effect inference and Pearl-do-calculus framing.
  • BioHub and encapsulation programs. Next-generation islet-delivery architecture — the omental biological scaffold, encapsulation strategies, and their interaction with continuing autoimmune and alloimmune pressure — moving from preclinical to clinical with dense preclinical signal.

Three capabilities remain underdeveloped in current analytic practice across islet-transplantation registries, including analyses applied to the DRI record to date:

  • Unified causal modeling of donor quality, recipient state, immunosuppression exposure, and long-horizon graft outcome — recovering the directed structure rather than pursuing marginal regression across each covariate independently.
  • Long-horizon graft-survival hazard decomposition with calibrated uncertainty — parsing the subject-level features driving decade-plus durability from the features driving early attrition.
  • Principled immunosuppression-regimen and BioHub-variant prioritization on a unified causal graph — rather than regimen-by-regimen empirical iteration.
The DRI record is a unique substrate: the longest continuous clinical-islet-transplant archive in the world, paired with an active BioHub translational program. The question is no longer whether islet replacement can produce insulin-independence — the DRI record demonstrates it — but which donor, recipient, regimen, and delivery-architecture combinations sustain the outcome over a decade and beyond. That is a problem of causal inference on a uniquely long-horizon dataset.

2 · What Manifold brings

Manifold is a causal-intelligence platform for detecting structural fragility and regime transitions in complex multi-signal systems. Three engine capabilities form the basis of the DRI-configured deployment:

  1. Causal discovery (Spirtes / PC algorithm) on the DRI transplant registry. Recovers the directed structure among donor quality, isolation yield, recipient HLA and autoantibody profile, immunosuppression exposure, and long-horizon graft outcome — the substrate for principled heterogeneous-recipient and heterogeneous-regimen inference.
  2. Criticality estimators feeding a composite fragility score (ΩF). Individual estimators are configured to registry-scale longitudinal data — long-horizon survival models, nonlinear mixed-effects C-peptide decay, heterogeneous-treatment-effect learners on regimen variants, Bayesian change-point on graft-compromise timing — with the composite score providing a stable integration layer.
  3. Pearl do-calculus and interdiction. Regimen and delivery-architecture prioritization — including BioHub-variant design — is framed as a maximum-attenuation intervention on the inferred causal graph, producing principled ranking of modifications rather than empirical sequential iteration.

5 · Proposed initial engagement (Phase 1)

The scope of the proposed Phase 1 engagement is defined to produce a deliverable of demonstrable analytic value to the DRI within a contained time and data footprint.

  • Primary dataset. DRI allogeneic islet-transplant registry: donor and recipient covariates, isolation yield and viability, immunosuppression regimen, longitudinal C-peptide AUC, insulin-dependence status, and graft-outcome at multi-year follow-up. Where available, retrospective BioHub preclinical and early clinical signal as complementary input.
  • Access path. DRI-internal Data Use Agreement and IRB, under the sponsorship of a DRI-affiliated principal investigator.
  • Deliverable. Per-subject ΩF trajectory integrating long-horizon survival, heterogeneous-treatment-effect, and change-point estimators. Graft-attrition hazard decomposition at 1, 5, and 10-year horizons. Pearl-do-calculus ranking of immunosuppression and delivery-architecture modifications predicted to most attenuate long-horizon attrition.
  • Success criteria. Long-horizon hazard model with calibration and discrimination matching or exceeding the DRI's pre-specified biostatistical analyses; identification of at least one durable-response subgroup decomposable along ΩF pillars; interdiction-engine regimen and BioHub-variant rankings interpretable and actionable by the DRI clinical team.
  • Timeline. Twelve to fourteen weeks from data access.
  • Excluded from Phase 1 (reserved for the Phase 2 prospective arm): prospective BioHub or encapsulated-variant trial design, live CGM ingestion, bespoke immunologic assays.

6 · Rationale and timing

  • Long-horizon data has no substitute. The DRI registry is the only islet-transplant archive of its duration and continuity. The analytic tools appropriate to long-horizon multi-covariate data — causal discovery, deep survival, heterogeneous-treatment-effect inference — have only recently matured to production grade against it.
  • BioHub is at the translational inflection point. The omental-scaffold, encapsulated, and next-generation delivery programs moving through the DRI translational pipeline have preclinical and early clinical signal; their prioritization is directly a causal-inference problem.
  • Replacement paradigm broadly has crossed proof-of-principle. Insulin-independence in early-phase Vertex VX-880 cohorts reinforces the field's focus on generalization — which donor, recipient, regimen, and architecture combinations sustain the outcome — which is the question the DRI registry is uniquely positioned to answer.
  • Relevant funding mechanisms are active. The Breakthrough T1D Cures program, the Helmsley Charitable Trust Restoring Insulin Production stream, and NIH NIDDK all have active Requests for Proposals spanning islet-transplantation analytics.
  • The platform is production-ready. Manifold's causal, criticality, and interdiction engines are production-grade; configuration for the DRI registry and BioHub domains represents bounded, well-scoped work.

7 · Proposed partnership

Partnership with a Diabetes Research Institute principal investigator at the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine is sought for:

  1. Retrospective DRI islet-transplant registry and BioHub data access under the DRI's existing Data Use Agreement and IRB.
  2. Clinical co-authorship on the long-horizon graft-survival and regimen-interdiction benchmark manuscript.
  3. Co-application to Breakthrough T1D Cures, Helmsley Restoring Insulin Production, or NIH NIDDK for the Phase 2 prospective arm.
