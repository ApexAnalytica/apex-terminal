## The model

Apex acts as a small consultancy with a platform attached. The cash flow is straightforward:

- Apex sells the engagement (brand, sales pipeline, contracting, billing).
- You deliver the engagement (your hours, your expertise, your judgment on the deliverable).
- Client pays Apex. Apex pays you a defined share. Apex retains the rest for infrastructure, Claude API costs, and platform engineering.

Specifically, the share splits I'm proposing:

| Offering | Price to client | Your share | Apex share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ΩF Mini-Audit** | $1,500 | $900 (60%) | $600 (40%) | Templated 5-day deliverable. Higher Apex margin because per-sale pipeline cost is high relative to ticket size. |
| **Causal Risk Assessment** | $7,500-15,000 | 65% | 35% | 2-3 week custom engagement. |
| **Model / Optimization Review** | $15,000-30,000 | 65% | 35% | 4-6 week deeper engagement. Daniel: RL / model interpretability. Georgios: quantum / collateral / continuous optimization. |
| **Expert network calls** (GLG / AlphaSights / Guidepoint) | Network rate ($400-1,500/hr realized after their cut) | 70% of net | 30% of net | You apply individually; Apex provides brand and coordinates which calls you take. |
| **Quant-on-Demand retainer** | $3,000-8,000/month | 65% | 35% | Recurring monthly access; we staff against retainers as they sign. |

The 65/35 mid-tier split is the headline number. Apex's 35% is what funds:
- Claude API credits (the infrastructure you're using to build with)
- Hosting, Supabase, Vercel, NVIDIA / AWS infrastructure
- Brynna's coordination time on contracts, scheduling, customer success
- Ongoing platform engineering investment that doesn't bill against client hours

If 35% feels too high, we talk about it. The number isn't sacred. What is sacred is that **some** Apex margin has to flow to platform infrastructure or this model collapses into "we're just a staffing firm" and the platform stops compounding.

## HSBC compliance considerations — for Jeremy

Before Jeremy's first billable engagement, the following needs to happen:

1. **Review of your HSBC employment contract** — specifically the moonlighting / outside-employment / non-compete / IP-assignment clauses. We need to know what's permitted, what requires disclosure, and what's prohibited.
2. **Confirmation of HSBC's external-engagement disclosure process.** Most large banks require pre-approval for paid external work. We follow whatever process HSBC has, in writing, before any engagement starts.
3. **A short list of conflict-of-interest scenarios we agree to avoid in writing:** no engagements with direct HSBC competitors on similar product lines, no HSBC clients without explicit approval from both sides, no use of HSBC time / IP / data / facilities for Apex work, no HSBC-confidential information used in any deliverable.
4. **Expert network platform setup as the safest first revenue lane.** Most banks specifically permit expert network calls under standard disclosure rules — that's where Jeremy starts, with the lowest compliance friction. Direct client engagements (Causal Risk Assessment, Model Review) only after the contract review confirms what's permitted.
5. **Framework / authorship work isn't gated** by the compliance review — that's intellectual property you developed and continue to develop independently of HSBC employment. Publications, preprints, ΩF framework refinement can begin immediately.

I'll absorb the cost of getting your contract reviewed by an employment lawyer who specializes in financial-services moonlighting. The cost of doing this right (a few hundred dollars, a few days) is dramatically less than the cost of getting it wrong.

**Until your HSBC compliance review clears, your participation is platform-engineering and authorship-only. The 90-day commitment clock starts when compliance clears, not before.**

If the compliance review surfaces something that makes external engagement unworkable while you're at HSBC, the structure still has a meaningful place for you — equity participation, framework authorship, platform engineering — without requiring billable hours.
