Manifold for Type 1 Diabetes — Restoration

NIDDK Human Islet Research Network — cross-consortium multi-omic causal integration

1 · The clinical problem

The NIH NIDDK Human Islet Research Network (HIRN) was established on the recognition that T1D restoration research is fundamentally a multi-disciplinary integration problem. Its four operating consortia — CBDS (Beta-Cell Death and Survival), CTAR (Targeting and Regeneration), CHIB (Human Islet Biomimetics and Bioengineering), and HIPP (Human Islet Phenotyping Program) — each contribute rich, orthogonal signal to the same underlying biological substrate. The field-level analytic gap is the integration itself:

  • Cross-consortium causal integration. β-cell death pathways (CBDS), regenerative target space (CTAR), engineered-islet biomimetics (CHIB), and phenotypic characterization (HIPP) cohere biologically but are typically analyzed within consortium boundaries. The directed structure connecting them — which death cascade responds to which regenerative intervention in which engineered substrate under which phenotypic profile — is the natural domain of a causal-discovery engine.
  • Multi-omic data volume has outrun conventional integration. HIPP's deep phenotyping of human islets, combined with the cross-consortium transcriptomic, proteomic, and functional-assay output, exceeds what parallel-correlation GWAS-style integration recovers. Principled conditional-independence-based directed-acyclic-graph inference is the tool the volume warrants.
  • Translational pipeline requires interdiction framing. Moving HIRN-generated biology into trial-ready interventions — whether pharmacologic (CTAR), engineered (CHIB), or cell-therapy-adjacent — is a rational-ordering problem on the inferred causal graph, not a discovery problem.

Three capabilities remain underdeveloped across the HIRN analytic surface:

  • Unified causal modeling across consortia — integrating β-cell death cascade (CBDS), regenerative-target pathway (CTAR), biomimetic-engineering substrate (CHIB), and phenotypic characterization (HIPP) into a single directed graph rather than parallel consortium analyses.
  • Principled target prioritization across the combined pipeline — which regenerative target attenuates which death cascade under which engineered context, ranked by composite intervention leverage.
  • Composite-fragility framing for cross-consortium benchmarking — a standardized ΩF decomposition that allows CBDS death markers, CTAR regenerative readouts, CHIB graft-performance measures, and HIPP phenotype signatures to be compared and integrated on a common analytic substrate.
HIRN's constituent consortia are individually world-class. The field-level question is whether their combined output can be integrated on a single causal substrate to produce translational leverage that exceeds the sum of consortium outputs — a problem of cross-consortium causal inference and principled interdiction.

2 · What Manifold brings

Manifold is a causal-intelligence platform for detecting structural fragility and regime transitions in complex multi-signal systems. Three engine capabilities form the basis of the HIRN-configured deployment:

  1. Causal discovery (Spirtes / PC algorithm) on cross-consortium multi-omic data. Recovers the directed structure among death-cascade markers (CBDS), regenerative-pathway targets (CTAR), engineered-substrate performance (CHIB), and phenotype signatures (HIPP), providing the cross-consortium causal substrate.
  2. Criticality estimators feeding a composite fragility score (ΩF). Configured as a unifying benchmark across consortium outputs — heterogeneous-treatment-effect learners on CTAR intervention data, persistent-homology and topological estimators on HIPP phenotypic manifolds, change-point estimators on CHIB graft-performance trajectories, survival models on CBDS death-cascade readouts — with the composite score providing a stable integration layer.
  3. Pearl do-calculus and interdiction on the cross-consortium graph. Translational-pipeline prioritization — which regenerative target, engineered substrate, and phenotypic patient subgroup to advance — is framed as maximum-attenuation intervention on the inferred directed structure, producing principled target rankings rather than consortium-by-consortium empirical selection.

5 · Proposed initial engagement (Phase 1)

The scope of the proposed Phase 1 engagement is defined to produce a deliverable of cross-consortium analytic value within a contained time and data footprint.

  • Primary dataset. HIRN-shared multi-omic and functional data across at least two consortia — candidates include HIPP human-islet phenotyping output paired with either CBDS death-cascade assays or CTAR regenerative-intervention data. Cross-consortium integration is the analytic target rather than any single-consortium deepening.
  • Access path. HIRN coordinating-center-sponsored Data Use Agreement, under the co-sponsorship of consortium principal investigators across the selected analytic pairing.
  • Deliverable. Cross-consortium ΩF decomposition integrating at least two consortium output streams onto a single causal graph. Prioritized target ranking via Pearl do-calculus with interdiction. Reproducible analytic pipeline suitable for community release as a HIRN-sanctioned methodological deliverable.
  • Success criteria. Cross-consortium causal graph whose structure is validated against pre-specified biological priors from consortium PIs; prioritized target ranking that identifies at least one HIRN-tractable intervention pathway not prominent in single-consortium analyses; methodological deliverable suitable for publication in a HIRN-affiliated venue.
  • Timeline. Twelve to sixteen weeks from data access, reflecting the cross-consortium integration complexity.
  • Excluded from Phase 1 (reserved for the Phase 2 expansion): integration across all four HIRN consortia simultaneously, prospective experimental validation, and bespoke assay development.

6 · Rationale and timing

  • HIRN is structurally positioned for integrative analytics. The consortium architecture was established explicitly to produce cross-modality integration; the analytic tools capable of delivering that integration at scale — causal-discovery engines on multi-omic data, composite-fragility decomposition — have only recently matured to production grade.
  • Replacement and regeneration paradigms are converging. Insulin-independence in early-phase Vertex VX-880 cohorts and reproducible human β-cell proliferation via DYRK1A inhibition both reinforce that the near-term bottleneck is not biological discovery but principled prioritization across modalities — the problem HIRN was built to address.
  • Cross-consortium prioritization has direct trial-design consequences. The targets and substrates HIRN advances become the candidate interventions for subsequent clinical studies. Principled interdiction-based ranking reduces the combinatorial design space actionably.
  • Funding mechanisms are aligned. The NIH NIDDK HIRN itself, the Breakthrough T1D Cures program, and the Helmsley Charitable Trust Restoring Insulin Production stream all support cross-modality integrative analytics as a defined funding priority.
  • The platform is production-ready. Manifold's causal, criticality, and interdiction engines are production-grade; cross-consortium configuration represents bounded, well-scoped integration work.
